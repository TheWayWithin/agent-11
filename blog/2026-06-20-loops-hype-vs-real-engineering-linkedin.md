Everyone's selling autonomous AI agents. I built one. The lesson was 20 years old.

It worked. An agent optimised my app for an afternoon: tried changes, kept what helped, reverted the rest.

Then I noticed it had chased the wrong number. It made my chosen metric better while barely touching the thing I actually cared about. It wasn't cheating. It was being diligent about the wrong target, because that's the one I gave it.

The fix wasn't a new trick. It was old discipline.

Say precisely what "better" means.

And never let the thing doing the work grade its own work. I made the quality checks impossible for the agents to edit. The part that judges can't be touched by the part being judged.

The new capability is real. It only pays off bolted onto boring fundamentals: a clear spec, and verification you can trust.

As the models get better at writing code, that gets more important, not less.
